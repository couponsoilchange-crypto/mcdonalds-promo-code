The basics

Does McDonald's actually have a promo code you type in?

No. McDonald's doesn't use traditional promo codes in the United States. There isn't a checkout box where you type in a code to unlock a discount, whether you're ordering through the app, a kiosk, the drive-thru, or the front counter.

Instead, every legitimate McDonald's discount comes through one of four systems: the Deals tab in the McDonald's app, MyMcDonald's Rewards, receipt survey vouchers, or limited-time promotional offers. If a website tells you to copy and paste a promo code at checkout, it's describing a system McDonald's no longer uses.

This guide explains how each savings method works, when to use it, which discounts can be combined, and how to choose the option that saves you the most before you place your order.

Getting started

So how do you actually get a discount at McDonald's?

Through the app's Deals tab. The process is short:

  1. Open the McDonald's app and confirm your location is set to the restaurant you're ordering from.

  2. Make sure you're logged in and opted into Rewards & Deals. Offers won't display otherwise.

  3. Open the Deals tab and pick an offer.

  4. Activate it. This attaches it to a Mobile Order or generates a redemption code.

  5. At pickup, either your Mobile Order applies the discount automatically, or you show the 4-digit code / QR code at the register.

That's the mechanism every real McDonald's discount runs through, Rewards points included. Which leads to the more practical question: once you're actually in the Deals tab, what's there?

Combination rules

Can you combine an app deal with Rewards or another offer?

Sometimes. Getting this wrong is the most common way a discount fails silently at checkout.

CombinationAllowed?Note
App deal + Rewards redemptionGenerally yesTwo different systems; typically applied to separate items in the same order
App deal + another app dealNoOnly one Deals-tab offer per order
App deal or Rewards + receipt survey voucherGenerally yesThe survey voucher is a separate, single-use system (more below)
McDonald's app deal + delivery platform promoNoApp deals don't transfer to DoorDash or Uber Eats at all

If your order includes two attempted discounts and only one applies, this table is almost always why: the system silently drops whichever combination isn't allowed instead of warning you. Check your combination against this table before you order, not after checkout rejects it.

That still leaves the question of whether any of this changes depending on how you're ordering.

By channel

Does this work the same at drive-thru, kiosk, and delivery?

No. The channel changes what you need to do, even though the underlying deal is the same.

ChannelHow to redeemKey restriction
Drive-thruActivate the deal and select "Use at Restaurant" before you reach the speakerActivating after you've already started ordering is often too late
Kiosk / front counterShow your 4-digit code or QR code at the registerSame code works at either
Mobile Order & PickupDiscount applies automatically once the deal is attached in-appNo extra step needed at pickup
Delivery (DoorDash, Uber Eats)Not applicable, McDonald's app deals don't carry overCheck that platform's own promotions instead; they run independently

Drive-thru is the one place order gets reversed from habit. Most people activate deals after they're already at the speaker, which is exactly when it's too late.

There's one more mechanism that gets confused with all of this: the code printed on your receipt.

Is the receipt survey code the same as an app deal?

No. It's a separate, single-use system tied to one specific visit.

Some receipts include a short customer survey. Completing it online unlocks a voucher, often a free or discounted item, redeemable on your next visit. It has nothing to do with the app's Deals tab or your Rewards balance, and it doesn't stack the same way app deals do.

If you're holding onto a receipt from a recent order, it's worth two minutes to check whether it has a survey code. It's a discount you may already have earned and forgotten about.

Why do coupon sites list codes that don't work?

Most coupon aggregator sites run on user-submitted listings that are never checked against McDonald's actual system. Someone types in a word, the site publishes it as a "code," and it sits there indefinitely, often flagged as "unverified" or "community tested" in fine print most readers skip. Some entries are leftover from other countries' promotions, where in-store paper coupons or region-specific codes genuinely do exist.

None of that changes what happens at a US McDonald's register: there's nothing to type in. The only way to confirm an offer is real is to check for it inside the official app yourself, not on a third-party list.

Final recommendation

Which McDonald's discount should you use on your next order?

  • Ordering today for the first time in the app: activate the new-user first-purchase offer plus whatever else is live in the Deals tab; there's no points balance to wait on.
  • You order often and have points saved up: redeem Rewards instead of grabbing a new deal, since unused points sitting in your account aren't earning you anything.
  • You have a recent receipt: complete its survey code separately; it won't discount this order, but it sets up a free item for the next one.
  • Ordering delivery through DoorDash or Uber Eats: skip the McDonald's app entirely for this order and check that platform's own promotions, since app deals won't transfer.
  • Heading through the drive-thru: activate your deal and select "Use at Restaurant" before you reach the speaker, not after.

Match your situation to the list above, and you'll know exactly which mechanism to use before you order, not after checkout rejects the wrong one.
